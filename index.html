UX / UI Case Study & Rebrand

The Official parkrun App: Reimagined

Bringing the parkrun official brand identity to life while streamlining UX, redesigning UI, improving accessibility (WCAG AA), and introducing safe social friendship features.

Role Lead UX/UI Designer
Timeline 6 Months (Discovery to Launch)
Platform iOS & Android (Native)
Key Impact 70%+ Satisfaction & WCAG AA
Official parkrun Mobile App Interface System Rebranded 2024
01 / Context & Overview

Background & Problem Statement

1

Acquisition & Rebrand

Following parkrun's acquisition of the unofficial, custom-built "5K app," the application needed a comprehensive overhaul to align with official parkrun brand guidelines (colors, fonts, visual identity) without alienating its passionate user base.

2

Community Challenges

Grassroots community challenges (e.g., Alphabeteer, Stopwatch Bingo) lacked official brand alignment, had potential intellectual property issues, and suffered from accessibility barriers and unrefined UI tracking.

3

Social Safeguarding

The existing app lacked modern, secure two-way social consent mechanics, requiring a new social architecture to meet privacy, safeguarding, and anti-harassment standards.

Core Project Goals

  • Brand Alignment: Seamlessly rebrand the app, optimise bottom navigation, and establish a clear homepage dashboard.
  • WCAG Accessibility: Create official badge graphics, redesign progress tracking UI, and guarantee full WCAG accessibility.
  • Safe Social Model: Design an intuitive, secure friendship model (search, follow/follower management, and blocking) for fast finish-line interactions.

Target User Groups

Engaged Power Users

Patreon subscription members, ambassadors, event directors, and core parkrun staff.

Global Runners & Walkers

Casual and regular parkrunners across 20+ global territories searching for barcodes & stats.

Accessibility Group

Members of the internal Digital Accessibility User Group (blind, visually impaired, colorblind, neurodivergent).

02 / Process & Methodology

Discovery, Research & Testing

Step 1 / Quantitative Data & Competitors

Analytics & Heuristics

  • Analysed Firebase analytics to identify high-traffic app screens and drop-off points.
  • Conducted direct/indirect competitor benchmarking (Strava, Nike Run Club, Garmin).
  • Heuristic evaluations focused on social friendship mechanics and barcode lookup speed.
Step 2 / Surveys & Consultations

User Journey Mapping

  • Distributed targeted preference surveys to 500+ Patreon and community members (achieving a 64% response rate).
  • Evaluated dashboard widget priority and bottom navigation concepts.
  • Mapped user journeys specifically tailored for fast, time-sensitive post-event environments.
Step 3 / Categorisation & Identity

Badge & Challenge Systems

  • Categorised challenges into core types: Finishing events, Volunteering, or both.
  • Mapped parkrun official brand color coding to each challenge category.
  • Tested icon badge shapes (hexagons vs. rounded shapes) and layouts (Tile grid vs. List view).
Step 4 / Mixed-Methods Usability

Focus Groups & Prototypes

  • Facilitated 7 focus groups (15 staff, 17 ambassadors) across London, Leeds, Europe, and APAC.
  • Engaged 15 members of the Digital Accessibility User Group to evaluate contrast, text legibility, screen readers, and dark mode.
  • 1-on-1 usability testing with 15+ users using interactive high-fidelity Figma prototypes.

03 / Key Insights & Decisions

Design Rationale & Trade-Offs

5-Feature Nav Bar

Selected a 5-icon bottom navigation bar (Home, Extras, Events, Barcode, Profile) to guarantee 2-click access to core utilities.

3-Element "Trio" Dashboard

Despite a 47% vs 53% split, Design A1 ("Trio") was chosen because landscape cards offered superior legibility over crowded icons.

Map & Scope Rationalization

Top maps were less useful in low event-density regions. Custom widget ordering was deferred to V2 due to API constraints.

List View over Tile Grid

Despite a 54.5% Tile vs 45.5% List preference vote, List view was selected because inline challenge descriptions prevented excessive tap-depth for new users.

Softer Badge Visuals

Hexagonal badges were perceived as rigid and "off-brand". Shifted to rounded organic shapes with soft drop shadows.

WCAG Accessibility Actions

  • Fixed incomplete greyscale contrast (from 1.1:1 up to 3.5:1 WCAG standard).
  • Replaced punitive red progress bars with encouraging vibrant amber/orange.
  • Added explicit screen reader programmatic labels (e.g., "Volunteer challenge - in progress - 45% complete").

Mutual Status Clarity

Usability testing revealed ambiguity between 1-way and 2-way follow states. Updated profile headers with explicit badges ("Follows You", "Following").

Safeguarding & Blocking UI

Refined blocking iconography and added mandatory confirmation modals with explicit copy to eliminate accidental blocking during fast finish-line scanning.

Final Design Artefacts

A

Central Dashboard & Nav

Rebranded central homepage dashboard featuring the 3-element landscape widget hierarchy and 5-icon bottom navigation bar.

B

Challenge Progress System

List view layout with rounded badges, high-contrast progress bars, inline descriptions, and post-event congratulatory modals.

C

Comprehensive Social Suite

Friend Search, Follower/Following management, Blocked user controls, and explicit mutual relationship status badges.

Results & Key Impact

  • Achieved over 70% satisfaction rating among engaged community testers during initial rebrand evaluation.
  • Prominent homepage placement of the parkrun Planner directly increased user feature engagement post-launch.
  • Delivered a safer, WCAG-compliant social experience with minimal software bugs upon rollout.

Reflections & Next Steps

Customization Roadmap: Future roadmap items should re-evaluate customizable dashboard widgets when dev resources allow.
Community Communication: Proactive community messaging is critical prior to major UI rollouts to mitigate change-resistance.
Hardware Screen Reader Testing: Accessibility testing must transition from static PDF mockups to native live screen-reader testing on real hardware.
